# Deepfakes, Misinformation & Generative AI Risk




# Synthetic Content


*What Is Synthetic Content?* 

Synthetic content is digital material created by AI or significantly altered using AI tools

It may be entirely generated from a prompt, or a manipulation of existing authentic content


**Synthetic content spans every media format:** 

• Text — emails, reports, news articles, legal documents, social media posts

• Images — photographs, ID documents, faces, scenes that never existed

• Audio — voice clones, fake phone calls, fabricated quotes

• Video — deepfakes, manipulated recordings, synthetic talking-head video





# Misinformation vs. Disinformation

*MISINFORMATION* 

False or inaccurate information shared without intent to
deceive

*DISINFORMATION*

Deliberately false or misleading content created and
shared with intent to deceive



# Secure Processes Beat Perfect Detection



*Process Controls That Resist Deepfake Fraud

Out-of-Band Verification

When any unusual request arrives — by any channel, from any apparent sender —
verification must occur through a separate, pre-established channel. Call back on a
known number. Never use contact info from the suspicious communication




Dual Approval for High-Value Transactions

No single employee should authorize or execute a large wire transfer alone,
regardless of who appears to be asking. Two independent approvers must both
confirm, through separate channels

Trusted Channel Policy

Define in writing which channels are authorized for financial instructions. Company-
email-to-company-email with standard logging: authorized. Video call followed by
Gmail: not authorized. Make this explicit in policy, not implied.


Pre-Shared Verification Secrets

For high-risk roles, establish a pre-shared code or phrase that must be provided for
any out-of-process request. This is immune to deepfakes — a synthetic voice cannot
know a secret it was never told.


# Content Provenance & Authenticity

*Content Provenance and Authenticity Approaches* 

Content Provenance

Documented history of where content originated and how it was modified. Most
reliable when established at creation — before any alteration occurs. A chain of
custody for digital media.



Metadata Analysis

Examining embedded file metadata (EXIF data, timestamps, device info) can reveal
editing history. Metadata is frequently stripped by social platforms and is
intentionally removed by sophisticated actors.


C2PA Content Credentials

The Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity (C2PA) standard embeds
cryptographically signed metadata at creation, recording who made the content,
when, and with what tool. Cameras and AI tools that support C2PA sign content at
capture.


AI Detection Tools

AI-trained models that detect synthetic content by identifying statistical artifacts —
unnatural blinking, pixel inconsistencies, frequency domain anomalies. Accuracy
varies. Adversarial optimization defeats many detectors. Never rely on a single tool.




# Generative AI Risk

*Generative AI Risk Category 1 — Information and Content* 

Inaccurate or Fabricated Information

AI produces plausible-sounding text that may be factually wrong — fabricated
citations, non-existent laws, incorrect specifications. Professional verification is
required before any AI output is relied upon.



Harmful Bias in Output

Generative AI reflects patterns in training data — including historical biases. Outputs
may contain stereotyped or discriminatory representations that create reputational,
legal, or operational harm when deployed.



Misinformation and Disinformation at Scale

Generative AI dramatically reduces the cost of producing false or misleading
content. Organizations face both the risk of AI-generated disinformation targeting
them and liability for misinformation they inadvertently generate.

Overreliance

When employees accept AI outputs without verification, errors compound and
professionals lose the independent judgment needed to evaluate information.
Overreliance in security assessments is a specific cybersecurity risk.



**Generative AI Risk Category 2 — Security and Operation**


Insecure Generated Code

AI-generated code may contain security vulnerabilities — hardcoded credentials,
injection risks, insecure dependencies, incorrect auth logic. All generated code
requires the same security review as human-written code



Shadow AI

Employees using unsanctioned AI tools outside organizational oversight create data
governance, confidentiality, and compliance risks that are invisible until an incident
occurs. Policy must define approved tools and permitted data inputs.



Social Engineering at Scale

Generative AI enables attackers to produce highly personalized phishing, voice
clones, synthetic relationship-building, and fake personas at industrial scale. Spear
phishing and vishing attacks become significantly more accessible

Adversarial Manipulation

AI systems can be manipulated through prompt injection, model poisoning, and
data extraction attacks. Organizations deploying AI in security contexts must
evaluate robustness against deliberate adversarial manipulation.




# Organizational Controls & Governance


*Controls That Reduce Synthetic Media Risk*



• Synthetic content is not automatically malicious — intent and impact determine harm

• Misinformation and disinformation require different response strategies — do not conflate them

• A deepfake is a technique, not automatically disinformation — classify with evidence, not assumption
