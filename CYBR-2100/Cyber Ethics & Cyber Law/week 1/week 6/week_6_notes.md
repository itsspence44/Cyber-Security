
# Governance vs. Operations

*A critical distinction that shapes how cybersecurity decisions get made* 





# Cybersecurity Operations vs. Cybersecurity Governance


**CYBERSECURITY OPERATIONS**

Investigating alerts and triaging incidents

Configuring systems and applying patches

Monitoring logs and dashboards




**CYBERSECURITY GOVERNANCE**

What are our organizational risk priorities?

How much risk are we willing to accept?

Who is responsible for what decisions?





# NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0

**GOVERN Function** 

Organizational  Context

Understanding what the organization does, what is critical to its mission, who

depends on it, and what legal or contractual obligations apply. Cybersecuritydecisions should always support the organization's actual purpose



**Risk Management Strategy***


Establishing how the organization thinks about and responds to cybersecurity

risk — including its risk appetite, its tolerance for specific risk types, and how risk decisions are documented and communicated.



**Roles, Responsibilities, and Authorities**

Defining who is responsible for what cybersecurity decisions, who has authority

to approve risk acceptance, and who must be consulted or informed. Ambiguityhere creates dangerous gaps





# Risk Treatment Options


**The Four Risk Treatment Options** 

Mitigate

Reduce the likelihood or impact of the risk. This is the most common response — implementing controls, patching systems, adding monitoring, or

strengthening authentication. Mitigation does not eliminate risk; it reduces it to an acceptable level.







**Avoid**

Stop doing the activity that creates the risk. If using a particular technology, process, or third-party service creates unacceptable risk and the activity is not essential, stopping it eliminates that specific risk.

Not always possible or practical, but often underused.






# Compliance vs. Security



*Compliance Requirements vs. Cybersecurity Risk Management*


Establishes specific, defined obligations the organization must meet

Often based on industry standards, law, or regulation (HIPAA, PCI- DSS, SOC 2) Asks: "Did we meet the required standard?"



CYBERSECURITY RISK MANAGEMENT

Asks what could happen to this organization and what to do about it

Forward-looking: what are the emerging and evolving threats? Prioritizes based on organizational context, not regulatory checkbox




# Governance Responsibility Mapping

Who recommends, who approves, who implements, who owns the risk?



**The Stakeholders in Organizational Cybersecurity Governance**


Governing body / Board of Directors — ultimate accountability for organizational risk at the highest level

CEO — organizational accountability for all risk decisions not delegated to others

CISO or Security Leader — recommends, advises, monitors, and communicates cybersecurity risk



**For Each Cybersecurity Decision** 


**Who Recommends?**

Who identifies the issue, analyzes options, and brings a recommended course of

action to the decision maker? Usually the security team, but may involve IT, legal, or compliance depending on the decision type.


**Who Owns the Risk?**

Who is accountable if the risk materializes? Risk ownership should sit with the

person or role who benefits from the activity creating the risk — usually the business unit or operations leader, not the security team.



**Who Approves?**

Who has the authority to authorize the decision — fund the control, accept the risk, or direct the response?

Authority must match accountability. Approvers must be able to commit organizational resources.
