# What Is Critical Infrastructure?
*Scope, stakes, and why a cyber incident here is different from other incidents* 


Critical infrastructure supports the services and functions that communities and society depend upon to function


**CISA identifies 16 critical infrastructure sectors — examples include:** 

Energy — electric grids, oil and gas pipelines, fuel distribution

Water and Wastewater — treatment plants, distribution systems

Communications — internet, telephone, broadcast, satellite

Healthcare and Public Health — hospitals, emergency services, blood supply

Financial Services — banking, payment systems, market infrastructure

# Why Critical Infrastructure Incidents Are Different


**Public Safety Consequences**

Failure of power, water, or healthcare systems can directly endanger lives — not

just inconvenience people or cost money. The stakes of a wrong decision are measured in human welfare


**Government and National Security Implications**

Attacks on critical infrastructure may constitute acts of aggression under

international law, trigger national security responses, and involve federal agencies with authorities that go beyond normal incident response.




**Essential Services Disruption**

Communities cannot easily substitute for electricity, clean water, or emergency

services. Disruption causes cascading harm that extends far beyond the primary organization affected


# Stakeholders in a Critical Infrastructure Incident

The affected organization — utility, hospital, water authority, financial institution

Customers and the public — people who depend on the affected service

Other critical infrastructure sectors — those affected by cascading failures

Local and state government — emergency management, public communication, regulatory oversight
